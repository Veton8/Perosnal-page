I've built and run customer success organizations at Intel, Oracle Cloud, Microsoft Azure, VMware, and AWS. Each company, each era, taught me something different about what "customer success" actually means. But nothing has shifted the landscape like AI.

## The Old Playbook

For two decades, customer success was fundamentally reactive. A customer had a problem, we solved it. A customer was at risk of churning, we intervened. The best CS organizations got ahead of the curve with health scores and usage data, but the model was still: wait for a signal, then act.

The metrics were straightforward — Net Promoter Score, renewal rates, expansion revenue. And they worked. At [VMware](/experience/vmware-customer-success/), we increased NPS by 3+ points and onboarded 200+ customers onto a brand-new product line. At [Oracle Cloud](/experience/oracle-oci-customer-experience/), customer-centric execution helped grow the business from $200M to over $1.25B ARR. The fundamentals were sound.

## What AI Changes

AI doesn't just improve the old playbook — it rewrites it. Here's what I see changing:

**From reactive to predictive.** AI models trained on usage patterns, support tickets, and engagement data can identify at-risk accounts before anyone picks up the phone. The CS team's job shifts from firefighting to strategic intervention.

**From one-size-fits-all to personalized at scale.** When I ran CS for Microsoft's top 200 global accounts — Banco Santander, Air Canada, Bank of America — each account got a tailored engagement model because we had 500+ people dedicated to the effort. AI makes that level of personalization possible for every customer, not just the top 200.

**From product support to outcome engineering.** When the product itself uses AI — when it learns, adapts, and changes behavior — the customer success team has to understand the product at a fundamentally deeper level. You're not onboarding someone onto a static tool. You're helping them build a relationship with a system that evolves.

## What Stays the Same

The tools change. The principle doesn't. Customer success has always been about one thing: does the customer win? If they do, the revenue follows. If they don't, no amount of tooling saves you.

At [Intel](/experience/intel-customer-success/), we were building AI-as-a-Service — making AI more accessible by providing an alternative to NVIDIA's hardware/software stack. The technology was cutting-edge. But the customer success motion was ancient: understand the customer's problem, prove the value, earn the renewal.

## What I'd Tell a CS Leader Starting Today

Build your team around people who understand both the technology and the customer's business. Invest in data infrastructure before you invest in AI tooling — you can't predict churn if your data is a mess. And never forget that the most powerful customer success tool is still a human being who picks up the phone and says, "I noticed something — can we talk about it?"

The AI era doesn't replace customer obsession. It amplifies it.
