Granada is the kind of city that keeps surprising you with what's directly above your head. Look up — at a doorway, a ceiling, an arch — and you find centuries of geometry packed into a single square metre.

## The Alhambra

The Alhambra is the obvious centerpiece, and it earns it. The carved muqarnas ceilings, the calligraphic panels, the play of water through the patios — it's a complete environment, designed to be experienced unhurriedly. Going slowly is not optional; it's part of how the architecture works on you.

## Walking Granada

Beyond the Alhambra, Granada rewards walking. The Albaicín — the old Moorish quarter on the hill across from the palace — is a maze of narrow streets, whitewashed houses, and miradores (viewpoints) that frame the Alhambra at golden hour. The stone gates, archways, and water channels at the foot of the city are quieter places to spend an afternoon, with the same craftsmanship you find inside the palace, just weathered by another few centuries.

## Why It Lands

Spain's Andalusia carries a layered history — Roman, Visigothic, Moorish, Christian — that doesn't sit in separate boxes; it's stacked into the same wall. For someone with Cairo roots, the Moorish layer is unmistakably home, expressed through a different cuisine and a different language but with the same vocabulary of arches, courtyards, and water.
