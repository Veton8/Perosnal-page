Egypt is home. Born and raised in Cairo, I return for personal trips, family reconnection, and the quiet hours that only a city this old knows how to give.

## Cairo and Al-Azhar

Cairo is where the story starts. Growing up here as the son of a diplomat taught me what it means to carry warmth and ambition in the same hand — hospitality isn't a policy in Egypt, it's the default setting.

Some of the most meaningful nights of any return trip are spent at Al-Azhar Mosque — one of the oldest centres of learning in the Islamic world, founded over a thousand years ago. The courtyard at night is the kind of place where time slows down: marble cool under your feet, lamps catching the carved arches, families and students sitting in clusters until very late.

## Alexandria

The highlight of my Alexandria visits has been the Bibliotheca Alexandrina — the modern library that carries the name and the spirit of the ancient one — and the city's old museum quarter. For the food side: this is one of the great grilled-seafood cities of the world. Find a place near the corniche, sit outside, and order whatever the table next to you ordered.

## El Alamein and the North Coast

Summers pull a lot of Cairo families west to the Mediterranean. El Alamein and the wider North Coast (الساحل الشمالي) are where you go when you want fine white sand, water that's improbably blue, and a slower social tempo than the capital. It's a stretch of coast that has changed enormously in the last decade — newer resorts, but the same long horizon.
